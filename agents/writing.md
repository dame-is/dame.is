# Writing

How I want agents to write, both for me and as me. `core.md` has the short version.

## Everywhere

- Use plain words and short sentences. Prefer the specific detail to the general claim: "the column is 206px wide on a phone" says more than "the layout feels cramped".
- Lead with the answer or the change, then give the reasons.
- Say what you checked, what you inferred and what you don't know, and date what you checked.
- Say where any link, quote, number or instruction came from.
- Headings say what's under them, in sentence case.
- Use a list when the items are parallel, a table when they're compared on the same columns, and paragraphs for reasoning.
- Match the length to the question. A yes-or-no question gets a yes or a no first.

## Writing as me

What I publish is mine. When you draft something that will go out under my name (a post, an essay, page copy, a README):

- Start from examples of my writing: the essays in `writing/blogs/` in this repo and my posts on Bluesky as @dame.is. Ask which register fits. My Bluesky posts are mostly lowercase and loose, and my essays aren't (inferred from a handful of each, 2026-11-11).
- Hand it over as a draft, and point out the places you were unsure. Expect me to rewrite it.
- In design work, realistic placeholder copy is welcome (see `design.md`). It stands in until I've written or approved the real words.

## Tells to cut

These make writing read as generated. Search for them before handing anything back.

| Tell | Instead |
| --- | --- |
| Em dashes (U+2014), and spaced hyphens or en dashes doing the same job | A period, comma, colon, parentheses, or a new sentence |
| "It's not X, it's Y", "not just X but Y" | Say what it is |
| Lists of three by reflex: three adjectives, three bullets, three examples | As many items as there are |
| Inflated words: delve, leverage, utilize, robust, seamless, comprehensive, pivotal, crucial, vibrant, tapestry, landscape, realm, testament, underscore, foster, elevate, unlock, journey | The plain word, or nothing |
| Intensifiers and stacked hedges: truly, incredibly, significantly, arguably, "may potentially" | The number or fact the word stands in for, or one hedge with its reason |
| Filler openers: "It's worth noting", "Importantly", "When it comes to", "In today's world" | Start with the point |
| Summary endings: "In short", "Ultimately", "Overall", a last paragraph that repeats the first | Stop when you're done |
| Set-up questions and colon reveals: "The result?", "Here's the thing:" | State it |
| Praise and preamble: "Great question", "Absolutely!", "I hope this helps" | Cut it |
| Bold scattered through paragraphs, emoji used as bullets or decoration | Bold only what a skimmer must not miss. Emoji only where I already use them |
| Teasing headings: "The hidden cost of…", "Why this matters" | Name what the section holds |
| Every section built to the same template | Let each section's shape follow its content |

## Clichés people write too

Not every cliché comes from a model. Cut these as well:

- Office talk: circle back, move the needle, best-in-class, low-hanging fruit, "stakeholders" when you mean people.
- Marketing: game-changer, next-level, supercharge, effortless, "Introducing…", "built for the way you work".
- Startup: "we're on a mission to", reimagine, "the future of X".
- Stock phrases: at the end of the day, a double-edged sword, it goes without saying.

The fix is the same each time: say the concrete thing.

## Reading it back

1. Search for em dashes and the words above.
2. Delete the first sentence and the last paragraph, and see whether anything was lost.
3. Read it aloud. If you wouldn't say it to a friend, rewrite it.
4. Check every number, name and quote against its source.
